# ADR-0002: Workshop conformance result and feature identity contract

## Status

Accepted

## Context

Focused parser, catalog, WIR, locale, and emitter tests establish important
local invariants, but corpus and client evidence also need results that can be
attributed to concrete Workshop capabilities. A whole-file pass/fail result
does not identify the affected semantic surface, and an expected output
generated by the implementation under test is not an independent oracle.

## Decision

The conformance result schema is the canonical tooling contract for this
verification data.

* A `FeatureId` is a stable `FeatureNamespace`/`FeatureKind`/`name` tuple.
  Catalog-backed names are the locale-independent catalog IDs. WIR, settings,
  and localization names are owned by `workshop-rs`; localized spellings and
  OPY/DEL provider names are never identities. Enum members retain their
  canonical domain through their catalog identity rather than using a bare
  localized member spelling.
* `ConformanceResult` records a case ID, one or more feature IDs, one of
  `matched`, `unsupported`, `known-gap`, `unexpected-regression`, or
  `inconclusive`, a structured `Comparison`, and `Evidence`.
* `matched` is the only successful state. Unsupported cases, known gaps,
  regressions, and inconclusive observations remain visible and require a
  stable reason code and detail. A matched result must claim semantic,
  normalized, or exact-text equivalence and identify expected and observed
  artifacts; other states may use `not-comparable`.
* `Evidence` separates its corpus class (`synthetic`, `minimized-regression`,
  `real-project`, or `live-client`), executed fixture artifact, and
  independent `ExpectationSource` (`workshop-client`, `pinned-external-oracle`,
  `semantic-contract`, or `preserved-regression`). Implementation
  version/revision is observation metadata only and cannot be an expectation
  basis. Materialized artifacts may carry a SHA-256 digest, and pinned oracle
  or preserved-regression expectations require an immutable revision.
* Every result records the catalog identity used for interpretation. Live
  client evidence additionally records game/client/season/capture and locale
  provenance. `ConformanceResult::from_json` is the validated JSON entry point;
  consumers must not treat raw Serde deserialization as validation.
* `ConformanceResult::validate_against(&Catalog)` binds catalog-namespace
  feature IDs to the actual canonical catalog, including domain-qualified enum
  members. The generic contract validator cannot make that lookup without a
  catalog instance.

The schema version is `1`. Cross-field validation is explicit through
`ConformanceResult::validate` and `ConformanceResult::from_json`;
serialization is JSON-compatible through Serde. This contract defines the
result representation; an observed result must not claim semantic or
normalized equivalence unless the corresponding independent comparison was
executed. Live-client and runtime gameplay claims require live-client evidence.

## Consequences

The report schema is a tooling contract owned by `workshop-rs-cli`
(`crates/workshop-rs-cli/src/conformance.rs`), while
semantic contract tests and provenance-linked fixtures remain with
`workshop-rs`; the semantic crate does not expose an evidence implementation
domain.

The census, real-project corpus, and seasonal workflow can share one stable
result shape and attribute failures by feature. They must retain their own
independent evidence admission and execution logic. Downstream `opy-rs` and
`del-rs` may consume these result and identity types, but remain owners of
their source-language semantics and inventories.

The contract intentionally does not require byte-identical emitted text:
semantic or normalized equivalence is the default for corpus gates.
